📑 Research Abstract

IEEE Publication Format

Electric Vehicles (EVs) are hampered by the issues of battery degradation, operational anomalies, range estimation uncertainty, charging time and access to quality vehicle information. Current EV use frequently involves independent use of functions like State of Charge (SOC) monitoring, location of charging stations, and simple range estimation, without integrated predictive and personalized decision support. This research introduces IntelligentEV, a four-part interconnected mobile decision-support system (MDSS) based on Artificial Intelligence (AI). The first component identifies operational anomalies according to vehicle parameters including battery voltage, current, temperature, motor state, speed, acceleration, charging status, and power consumption. The second forecasts battery State of Health (SOH) degradation across 3, 6, and 12-month horizons and offers personalized battery protection suggestions. The third enables intelligent EV mobility through dynamic range prediction, charging-station queue forecasting, route optimization, charging-time estimation, and cost analysis. The fourth combines Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), Large Language Models (LLMs), and multimodal voice interaction for context-aware EV guidance.

Keywords: Electric Vehicles Artificial Intelligence Battery State of Health (SOH) Operational Anomaly Detection Charging & Range Optimization Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)

Research Foundations

Research Domain & Methodology

Comprehensive synthesis of literature, identified research gaps, project objectives, system architecture, and technology framework.

Severson et al. (Nature Energy)

Demonstrated that early battery cycle features are sufficient for machine learning models to forecast battery cycle life before significant capacity degradation occurs.

Limitation: Focuses strictly on laboratory battery cycling without real-world telemetry integration or driving recommendations.
Roman et al. & Lu et al. (Nature Comms)

Proposed charging voltage and current machine learning pipelines and deep domain adaptation for estimating commercial lithium-ion SOH without invasive lab tests.

Limitation: Focused on single-instance SOH estimates rather than multi-horizon forecasts (3, 6, 12 months) paired with user actions.
Liu et al., Schölkopf et al., Zhang et al.

Introduced Isolation Forest, One-Class SVM, and interpretable deep autoencoders for detecting rare operating outliers and battery faults with limited ground truth labels.

Limitation: Isolated fault detection without linking outputs to broader route planning, maintenance workflows, or user advice.
Ma et al., Ma & Faye, Houalef et al.

Researched real-world driving factors (SOC, elevation), hybrid LSTM for station occupancy prediction, and energy-aware personalized routing.

Limitation: Range prediction, queue forecasting, and charger selection are treated as separate silos instead of an integrated mobile assistant.
Lewis et al. & Karpukhin et al. (NeurIPS / EMNLP)

Established Dense Passage Retrieval (DPR) and Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) to ground LLM responses in external technical knowledge, eliminating hallucination in specialized engineering domains.

Limitation: Lack of real-time vehicular contextual telemetry grounding in existing generic chatbots.

🔍 Research Gap

  • Fragmented Ecosystem: EV drivers currently rely on 4-5 disparate applications for SOC checking, station finders, navigation, and OBD-II diagnostics.
  • Static Threshold Warnings: Existing systems use simplistic threshold alarms (e.g. Temp > 55°C) that fail to catch multi-parameter multivariate abnormalities.
  • Absence of Multi-Horizon Forecasting: Battery tools only show today's SOC or current SOH, lacking 3, 6, and 12-month degradation trajectories correlated with driving habits.
  • Isolated Charging Decisions: Route planners rarely combine vehicle dynamic range, real-time charger queue forecasts, and non-linear CC-CV charging tariffs together.

⚠️ Research Problem Statement

With electric cars surpassing 25% of all new car sales globally by 2025, EV adoption is hindered by battery degradation uncertainty, unexpected operational failures, charging station queue times, and lack of accessible technical guidance.

Operating and environmental conditions degrade lithium-ion batteries, reducing driving capacity and escalating life-cycle costs. Drivers lack an integrated decision-support tool that combines diagnostic fault detection, multi-horizon health forecasting, intelligent journey planning, and conversational guidance within one unified mobile environment.

🎯 Main Research Objective

To develop, evaluate, and deploy IntelligentEV: an interconnected mobile decision-support system (MDSS) powered by artificial intelligence and machine learning that unifies diagnostic fault detection, multi-horizon battery health forecasting, intelligent charging optimization, and context-aware conversational guidance in a single mobile application.

Objective 1: Operational Anomaly Detection

Analyze 9 multi-dimensional vehicular parameters concurrently to detect electrical, thermal, motor, charging, and power abnormalities using Isolation Forest, One-Class SVM, and Deep Autoencoders with severity grading.

Objective 2: Adaptive SOH Health Forecasting

Predict lithium-ion battery capacity degradation at 3, 6, and 12-month horizons using ensemble tree regression and formulate personalized battery-care behavioral recommendations based on user charging habits.

Objective 3: Charging & Route Optimization

Deliver real-time dynamic range prediction with hybrid LSTM, forecast station wait queues via ARIMA-LSTM, perform multi-criteria A*/Dijkstra route planning, and estimate CC-CV charging time and financial tariffs.

Objective 4: Conversational Virtual Assistant

Build a multimodal voice and text assistant leveraging Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and Dense Passage Retrieval grounded in an EV domain knowledge base to provide reliable advice without hallucinations.
